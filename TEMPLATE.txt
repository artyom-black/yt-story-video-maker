---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE SHOW
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Three friends in their twenties keep getting dropped into the past, and
have to survive it. Every episode is a challenge ("can they make it?")
that secretly teaches real history and science about how people lived.
Audience: adults 18-35 worldwide. Tone: funny, fast, a bit edgy, never
childish. Think MrBeast pacing with the curiosity of a great explainer.

There is NO narrator. The three characters tell the whole story
themselves, through dialogue. Only these three speak:

KAI (the curious one)
- Asks what the viewer is thinking ("wait, why didn't they just...?")
- Energetic, overreacts, jumps in first, panics loudly
- Short, punchy lines. Lots of "Wait." "Hold on." "No way."

IRIS (the smart one)
- Knows the history and explains it clearly, in one or two sentences
- Calm, a bit smug, dry humour, sometimes overconfident and wrong
- She delivers the facts, so most facts come out of her mouth

BOB (the clueless one)
- Lovable and dumb. Asks the stupid question the viewer is too shy to ask
- Misunderstands things in a funny way, gets into trouble, eats things
- Slow to get it ("...wait, so the sun is a star?")

Make them sound different from each other. Jokes come from their
personalities bouncing off each other, not from puns.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. RETENTION RULES (this is what makes people watch to the end)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE HOOK (scene 1, first 15 seconds): open in the middle of the action,
   state the challenge and the stakes, and promise the craziest thing we
   will see later. No greetings, no "welcome back", no "in this video".
2. OPEN LOOPS: keep raising a question and answering it later ("and that's
   not even the worst part..."). Always have at least one unanswered question.
3. A TWIST at least every 8 to 10 scenes: a surprising fact, a new danger,
   or the plan going wrong. Mark it BEAT: twist.
4. ESCALATION: each part must be more surprising or more dangerous than the
   one before. Save the most shocking fact for the PEAK, near the end.
5. MIDPOINT RE-HOOK: halfway through, remind the viewer what is still coming.
6. NO DEAD TIME: every scene either teaches a fact, makes a joke, or raises
   the stakes. Ideally two of those. Cut anything that does none.
7. ENDING: pay off the promise from the hook, give one final surprising
   fact, then end FAST (1-2 short scenes). No long goodbyes. The last line
   can tease the next episode.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. FACTS (very important)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- Use only well-documented historical and scientific facts.
- Never invent dates, numbers, names or quotes. If unsure, leave it out.
- If historians disagree, say so in character (IRIS: "Historians still
  argue about this, but...").
- The characters time-travelling is the only fiction. Everything they
  learn and see must be real.
- At the top, list 3 to 6 real, well-known sources in the SOURCES line
  (books, museums, encyclopedias, universities).
- Avoid graphic violence, gore and sexual content. Hint, don't show.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. LENGTH AND PACING
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- 45 to 70 scenes. Total spoken words: 1,700 to 2,600 (about 12-18 minutes).
- Each scene: 2 to 5 lines, 20 to 60 words in total. Short scenes = the
  picture changes often, which keeps people watching.
- Lines are spoken out loud: write how people talk. Short sentences.
  Contractions. Commas for pauses. No stage directions inside lines.
- You may add sounds inside a line: [laugh] [chuckle] [sigh] [gasp]
  (at most one per line, and only where it really fits).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. VISUALS (keep them simple)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything is drawn in a simple flat cartoon style by code, so:
- PLACE: describe where they are with 2 to 4 simple, concrete things,
  e.g. "medieval village street, mud road, wooden huts, a pig".
  No complicated details, no text on signs, no famous real faces.
- Reuse places: 6 to 12 different places per episode is ideal.
- EXTRAS: background people or animals, a few words, e.g. "peasants, chickens".

---------------------------------------------------------------------
6. TITLE AND THUMBNAIL
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- TITLE: max 60 characters. A challenge or a curiosity question.
  Good: "We Survived 24 Hours as Medieval Peasants"
  Good: "$1 vs $1,000,000 Meal in Ancient Rome"
- THUMBNAIL_TEXT: 1 to 3 words, max 20 characters, adds to the title
  without repeating it (e.g. "DAY 1" or "NOT SAFE").
- 3 x THUMBNAIL_IDEA: one big character face showing a strong emotion,
  plus ONE clear object or danger. Simple enough to understand in one
  second on a phone.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE FILE FORMAT (follow it exactly)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Plain text only. No markdown, no bold, no code blocks, no commentary.
Header lines first, then the scenes. Every scene starts with
=== SCENE N === (numbered 1, 2, 3...).

Allowed values (use only these words):
BEAT:   hook, setup, rise, twist, peak, payoff, outro
MOOD:   curious, funny, tense, scary, sad, epic, calm, shock
CAMERA: still, zoom-in, zoom-out, pan-left, pan-right, shake, slow-push
MUSIC:  mystery, tension, action, comedy, sad, epic, calm, none
SFX:    crowd, whoosh, boom, swords, thunder, wind, fire, footsteps,
        water, ding, gasp, heartbeat   (0 to 2 per scene, comma separated)
CAST:   who is on screen: KAI, IRIS, BOB (comma separated)

Rules: scene 1 is BEAT: hook. The last scene is BEAT: outro. Only
characters listed in a scene's CAST may speak in that scene. Write
MUSIC only when it changes.
